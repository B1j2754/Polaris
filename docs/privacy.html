Polaris — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 19 August 2026

Polaris is a stargazing app. It has no accounts, no sign-in, and no server of its own. Everything the app records about you stays on your device. I do not collect, receive, sell, or share your personal data.

What Polaris stores on your device

  • Location. With your permission, Polaris reads your device location to work out which objects are above you. You can also add observing sites by searching for a place name or entering coordinates yourself.
  • Photos. Photos you add from your camera or photo library are copied into Polaris's own storage, along with the capture time read from the photo's EXIF data and any note, target, or site you attach to them.
  • App data. Your sites, targets, sessions, and settings, held in a local database on the device.

None of this is uploaded anywhere by Polaris. It is included in your device backups only if you have iCloud or Android backup enabled, in which case Apple's or Google's privacy policy applies to that copy.

What leaves your device

  • Open-Meteo. To show cloud cover, humidity, and temperature for an observing site, Polaris sends that site's coordinates (rounded to roughly 100 m) to Open-Meteo. When you search for a place by name, that search text is sent to Open-Meteo's geocoding service. No identifier, account, or device ID is attached to these requests.
  • CDS (Strasbourg). To show a preview image of a deep-sky object, Polaris requests a sky-survey cutout from CDS hips2fits. That request carries the celestial coordinates of the object you are looking at, a fixed property of the object itself, not your location, and nothing else. No identifier, account, or device ID is attached.
  • Expo updates. Polaris is built with Expo and checks Expo's update service for app updates. That request includes basic technical details such as platform, app version, and update channel.
  • App stores. Apple and Google provide me with aggregate, anonymous download and crash statistics. I cannot identify individual users from them.

Polaris contains no analytics, no advertising, and no third-party tracking SDKs.

Permissions

  • Location (while in use). Used to compute the sky above your current position. Denying it is fine — you can add observing sites manually instead.
  • Camera and photo library. Used only when you choose to add a photo to your portfolio. Polaris never scans your photo library on its own.

You can withdraw any permission at any time in your device settings.

Deleting your data

Because all of your data lives on your device, deleting the app deletes it. Individual photos, sites, and sessions can be deleted inside the app. There is nothing for me to delete on my side, because I never hold a copy.

Children

Polaris is suitable for all ages and does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, children included.
